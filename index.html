OPEN THE BLACK BOX

A simple model.
Visible assumptions.

These are teaching assumptions, not measured Lake Bunyonyi tourism statistics. Change the inputs to explore relationships, not to predict actual visitor numbers.

How visitors arrive 01

Each run covers 30 days, starting on a hypothetical Monday. Daily interest = base interest × season × day factor, rounded to a whole person. Low / normal / peak season use 0.65 / 1.00 / 1.50. Weekdays use 0.92 and weekends 1.20, each with a fixed seeded variation between 0.85 and 1.15 (seed 42).

How beds and stays work 02

The lake starts with empty beds. The overnight share determines stay requests; the rest are day visitors with no capacity limit. One guest uses one bed for the chosen number of nights. Departures free beds before new arrivals. Requests beyond free beds are turned away and leave; there is no waiting list, booking, alternative lodging or conversion to a day visit. Guests with nights beyond day 30 remain in beds; their later spending is outside the window.

How spending and activities work 03

A person-day is one day visitor or one occupied bed on a simulated day. Spending = person-days × the input spending rate, including accommodation, food, transport and activities together. It is gross visitor spending, not profit or a measured local economic impact. Each person-day has one main activity: boat / island circuit 40%, shoreline 35%, viewpoints 25%. The first two counts are rounded; viewpoints receives the remainder. Routes are illustrative; travel times are not modelled.

Baseline, limits and interpretation 04

The fixed baseline uses 100 interested visitors/day, normal season, 60% overnight visitors, 2-night stays, 160 beds and UGX 120,000/person-day. Inputs allow 0–500 visitors/day, 0–100% overnight, 1–7 nights, 0–1,000 beds and UGX 0–500,000 spending. Average occupancy includes the empty opening period. Zero beds is shown as no bed capacity. No real weather, holidays, costs, queues, environmental thresholds or calibrated tourism data are included.
